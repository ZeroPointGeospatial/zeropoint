Most public bodies already hold a great deal of spatial data. The difficulty is that it sits in different systems and coordinate references, with attributes nobody has defined, collected for one project and never maintained. A map made from it looks fine until two layers are laid on top of each other.

Our work for counties and agencies is mostly about making that data dependable: one coordinate system, attributes with stated meanings, gaps filled from the field or from imagery, and a record of where every layer came from. From there the analysis follows: land use and change, access to services, exposure to flooding, or whatever the plan needs to answer.

We hand data over in open formats with a data dictionary, so it outlives the project and the people who made it.
