The cheapest time to find a problem with a site is before it is bought, and the second cheapest is before it is designed. A topographic survey is how that happens: it records the ground as it is, so the layout, the drainage and the foundations are designed for the site rather than for a flat sheet of paper.

We measure the levels and features the design team will need: contours and spot levels, buildings and walls, trees, roads and paths, drains, poles and manholes at the boundary. Where the site is open we may fly a drone for the surface and check it with ground measurements; under trees and along structures we measure directly.

Every drawing names its coordinate system and height datum, and the control points we leave are described so they can be found again. That matters most on projects that run for a year or more, when the setting-out team needs to work in exactly the coordinates the survey used.
