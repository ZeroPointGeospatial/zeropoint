The relief model of Kenya, painted with what covers the ground: forest in deep green, farmland in ochre, towns in brick red, and the shrubland and grassland that make up four fifths of the country in pale khaki and straw. The highlands stand out at once, green and ochre around Mount Kenya, the Aberdares, the Mau and the Lake Victoria basin, against the open drylands of the north and east and the bare shores of Lake Turkana.

The classes come from ESA WorldCover, a map of the world at 10 metres made from Sentinel satellite images of 2021, blended here into cells of about 250 metres so each keeps the mix of what grows in it. The shares are the classification's own, measured over the whole country: mixed smallholdings often read as tree cover or grassland from space, so take the cropland figure as the satellite sees it rather than a farm census.
